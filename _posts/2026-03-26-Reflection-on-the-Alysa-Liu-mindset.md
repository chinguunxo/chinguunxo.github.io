If you haven't heard the joke "Whoever Alysa Liu's therapist is, I need to have that person as my therapist," where were you? (jk, this will require a basic understanding of the recent pop culture and mainstream media.)

The snippet from the post-olympic interview of the 19-year-old Olympian Alysa Liu has inspired the old and young and has given rise to the term *"Alysa Liu Mindset."*

> "What is there to lose?... Every second you are there, you are gaining something. There is nothing to be lost. Nothing to complain about." - Alysa Liu, 2026.

Up until March, I had been fed the message that obsession is the key to success. If you care about something, you have to make sacrifices and go through painful trials. But if the thing you are caring about is hurting you so much, is it really the right thing to be doing? Of course, you should preserve and *keep fighting, right?* Let's try answering no and let's be 

> "Winning isn't all that, and neither is losing. It's just something that happens. What matters is the input and the journey." - Alysa Liu, 2026.

Do you enjoy the daily process, or are you working towards some persona you could be that you think would be nice, that it would earn you respect?

Obsession with continuing, not the outcome.
