This blog post is for anyone who is considering:
> - Starting Visanne  
> - Stopping it due to side effects  

## My Story

I was first introduced to Visanne during my first visit to an international hospital in China. It was honestly wonderful. Compared to my previous encounters with doctors, they took my pain seriously instead of dismissing it with advice like “drink more water” or “exercise more.” During that visit, I was diagnosed with heterogeneous thickening of the uterine wall, which is associated with adenomyosis. Although Visanne is officially prescribed for endometriosis, it is also used to manage adenomyosis-related pain because it suppresses the menstrual cycle. (This varies by individual, and some people still experience spotting.)

## Why I Stopped

For me, the side effects began after about a year of taking the pill. I became extremely unmotivated, irritable, and anxious, and I constantly felt the need to sleep. The turning point came when I found myself crying every single day and having suicidal thoughts over something as minor and stuoid as an organic chemistry midterm result. I also read and watched other women’s experiences online, and many reported similar mental health side effects. The timing varied widely—some experienced them within weeks, others after months or even years. It seems highly subjetcive. Even then, deciding to stop was not easy. All I could remember was standing on a bus in -20°C weather, in unbearable pain, trying to make my way home alone crawling.  

After discussing it with a doctor, I decided to stop taking Visanne. We decided on checking whether the pain has subsided or not. Also, I needed to know whether what I was experiencing was truly a side effect or something else.

## Why I Went Back on It

Within a week of stopping, I noticed the depression leaving my body. I stopped obsessing over small things and finally felt relaxed. For the first time in a long while, I felt genuinely *happy*.  

Period returned about a month after stopping the pill, and the pain was intense. I needed more painkillers than I used to, and even then, they barely helped. The pain lasted for four days. I either had to miss classes or show up with a heating pad, unable to focus because of all of the brain fog. It took me nearly three weeks to fully catch up with my coursework. But in the end, it was not worth constantly anticipating when the next episode of severe pain would strike or scrambling to figure out how to manage it alone.  

During my last cycle, my period started right during my statistics final exam. The painkillers I took had no effect, and about an hour into the exam, I stopped feeling my hands as my whole body went numb due to the pain. I just rushed to finish the exam, because I knew I would not be able to answer any questions for a minute. After the exam, I asked my professor if I could retake the exam, explaining that "I was in a lot of pain". He responded, “Anyone could say that and ask to retake the exam,” and a hard "no."  

I was left crying—not just out of anger, but because I realized I might have to live with this for the rest of my life. It is sad to think of a life trying to awkwardly explain to someone why I could not finish an important project or miss a holiday because of my *period*. I hate how much taboo and stigmatized it is. Planning my life around my cycle felt nearly impossible as a college student studying abroad. So, I decided to go back on the pill.

## What I Hope for the Future

I hope for better treatment options—ones that do not force a choice between physical pain and mental health.  

I hope for more understanding from people, especially in academic and professional environments, about what chronic pain conditions actually feel like.

And most of all, I hope to one day live without having to choose between two kinds of suffering constantly. Until then, I will work hard towards contributing to its research as much as I can.

*P.S. Also, on that final exam, I still passed with a good score*

*Update on June 20th, 2026*

> Hi, after three weeks of retaking Visanne, the mental side effects came back. This time it was incredibly harder to focus on anything, and I had constant tiredness and dizziness, no matter how well I rested. Two options: (A) Stay on Visanne to stop adeno growth and take antidepressants and xanax for the mental side effects; (B) Stop taking any medication. It has been incredibly difficult and scary to make any decision on this journey. However, the anxiety does get worse!!! It can reach a point where a small accident could trigger a cascade of unexplained anxiety and brain activity. As a young person *with a brain that is still developing,* I am choosing to keep my mind safe and stop all medications at this moment. I am scared of hurting, but I am even more scared of losing my sanity and drive in life.
