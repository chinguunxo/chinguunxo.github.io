There's a version of me that waits until everything is perfect before showing it to anyone. That version ships very little.

The alternative — building in public, sharing early, inviting feedback — feels vulnerable. But it's consistently more useful.

## The perfectionism trap

Perfectionism isn't really about quality. It's about avoiding judgment. If you never ship, no one can tell you it's not good enough.

But that protection comes at a cost: you also never find out what's actually useful to people, what you got wrong, or what you could have done differently with a little outside perspective.

## What "building in public" actually means

It doesn't mean sharing every commit or narrating every decision. It means:

- Letting people see work that's good enough, not finished
- Being honest about what you're trying to figure out
- Not waiting for a launch moment to exist

> Done is better than perfect. Shipped is better than polished.

## A practical note

Start small. Post the project before the landing page is done. Write the blog post before you have three examples. Share the tool before you've added every feature you imagined.

The feedback you get early is worth more than the polish you'd add in isolation.
