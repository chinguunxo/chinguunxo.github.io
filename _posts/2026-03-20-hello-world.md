Welcome to my mind!

(Honestly, how should I start this. Maybe I should go to the library to check if there is a guidebook on writing blog posts...)

Now, for those who are interested, let me *informally* introduce myself. My name is Chinguun, spelled *chin-goon*, or actually however you like it! I grew up in a family that held pretty traditional values, and sometimes it was nice, and sometimes it was not. I picked up English by watching cartoons, where I got a huge fraction of my humor from, and it's a thing I connect with lots of cool people. Many people meet and describe me as shy/timid but my close friends think I am funny so I call that a win. I can draw and paint with almost everything, but never ask me to draw something, please. I have been confused since 10th grade, and now I am a 20-year-old college student (a.k.a a less confused version of the 9th grader). On the way, I like to think I have **figured out** the following:

| We choose to do the things that help us make sense of our existence

Why did you choose your major? For me, it is to make sense of my existence. It is not from a fear of death but from the fear of not knowing and being blind.

| Have grace when it comes to differences

I have been in many different settings, culturally, ethnically, socioeconomically, religiously, and morally. I have laughed and cried with many, and will do the same with many more. So far, people have taught me these:

  *1. It's everyone's first time living*: Have kindness. Be patient. Hear them first,
  *2. Try your best to help, but if it keeps hurting, it is okay to let go*: Some people are being affected by factors beyond your best help. You can reassure them thousands of times, but if that person is not willing to change and is making you feel bad in the process...it's not your fault. It is not their fault either. It will hurt, but it will hurt less than staying longer. You know you tried your best. 
  *3. Some people are pathological liars and still can be good people*: As a person who has a strong value for honesty, **this throws me off**. People behave with the way that helped them survive, and perhaps twisting and exaggerating small (and big) things have helped them as a little kid. They choose to keep it.
  *-. Don't give up*: There are amazing people, opportunities, and minds waiting for you! You have to find them and aspire to be like them.

To be honest, I am still working on expressing my thoughts meaningfully, and I think the only thing I can do is try to get better at it every day. I enjoy reading Substack posts (my personal favorite is @ milks and cookies!) and listening to video essays, but what is the point of collecting thoughts if I do not express them?
