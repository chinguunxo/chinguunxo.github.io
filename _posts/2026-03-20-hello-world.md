# Welcome to my mind!

*(Honestly, how should I start this? Maybe I should go to the library and check if there’s a guidebook on writing blog posts...)*

Now, for those who are interested, let me *informally* introduce myself. My name is Chinguun, pronounced *chin-goon* — or honestly, however you like it! I grew up in a family that held pretty traditional values, and sometimes it was nice, and sometimes it wasn’t. I picked up English by watching cartoons, which is where a huge part of my humor comes from, and it’s something I connect over with lots of cool people.  

Many people meet me and describe me as shy or timid, but my close friends think I’m funny, so I’ll call that a win. I can draw and paint with almost anything, but please never ask me to draw something.  

I’ve been confused since 10th grade, and now I’m a 20-year-old college student *(a.k.a. a slightly less confused version of my 9th-grade self).* Along the way, I like to think I have **figured out** the following:

---

## We choose to do the things that help us make sense of our existence

Why did you choose your major? For me, it’s to make sense of my existence. It doesn’t come from a fear of death, but from the fear of not knowing — of being blind.

---

## Have grace when it comes to differences

I’ve been in many different settings — culturally, ethnically, socioeconomically, religiously, and morally. I’ve laughed and cried with many people, and I will do the same with many more. So far, people have taught me this:

1. **It’s everyone’s first time living**  
   Be kind. Be patient. Listen first.

2. **Try your best to help, but if it keeps hurting, it’s okay to let go**  
   Some people are affected by factors beyond what you can help with. You can reassure them thousands of times, but if they’re not willing to change and you’re getting hurt in the process... it’s not your fault. It’s not entirely theirs either. It will hurt, but it will hurt less than staying longer. At least you know you tried your best.

3. **Some people are pathological liars and can still be good people**  
   As someone who values honesty deeply, **this throws me off**. But people behave in ways that once helped them survive. Maybe twisting or exaggerating things helped them as a child, and they chose to keep it.

- **Don’t give up**  
  There are amazing people, opportunities, and minds waiting for you. You just have to find them — and aspire to be like them.
